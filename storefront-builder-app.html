The app · Free and open source · Android and web

Your commission page, without learning Markdown.

Fill in what you charge, drop in your art, and press copy. You get a finished page ready to paste into rentry.co, laid out properly, with tables that line up and prices that survive the paste.

Free, both of them, permanently. No account. No upload. Works with no signal. Open source.

You sell art. Not tables.

A lot of illustrators run their commission page on rentry.co or somewhere like it. Free, no account, nobody taking a cut. It is a genuinely good choice and it has exactly one problem: the page is written in Markdown.

So you end up learning table syntax at midnight, or paying somebody forty dollars to lay out a price list you could have described out loud in thirty seconds. That is what this removes.

It opens empty, because it is your page. Press See an example page on the first screen and a finished shop loads: twelve sections, three price lists, a gallery, shipping, returns and terms. Change anything you like. Nothing is saved anywhere but your own browser.

What using it looks like

It tells you when it had to compromise

This is the part that is different from a text editor. Every paste site renders a slightly different dialect of Markdown, so a page that is correct on one can be quietly wrong on another. The app knows what each site can actually do.

Where a site cannot do something, your page degrades to something that still reads properly, and you are told what it gave up and why, pointing at the section it happened in. It never silently drops your work and it never pretends a compromise did not happen.

Nothing you write leaves your device

There is no account, no server, and no upload. Your pages are stored in the app on your phone. The compiling and the preview all happen on the device, which is also why it keeps working with no signal at all.

You can save a backup file whenever you like, and open it again later, on this device or another one. That file is yours and it is plain text.

A page it actually produced

Not a mockup. A whole shop built in the app and pasted into the real site: rentry.co/q7nyo28n. Twelve sections, three price tables with a photo and a spec list on every item, a gallery, shipping bands, returns, and terms.

Every one of the 79 things the compiler asked the site to build came out the other side: 9 headings, 4 tables, 14 rows, 13 images, 22 bullets, 12 runs of bold, 5 links. The shop is invented, and the page says so on its first line.

What it does not do yet

Said here rather than discovered later.

  • It does not host your images. The image field takes a web address. Upload at imgur.com, which needs no account, and paste the address back. That is the flow most people are already using.
  • It knows two sites. rentry.co, and a portable setting that works almost anywhere else. More can be added, and each one has to be checked against the real site rather than its documentation.
  • One device at a time. Moving a page to another phone means saving a backup and opening it there. There is no sync and no account, which is the same decision as the privacy one above.
  • Tested on one handset. A Moto G7 on Android 10, thoroughly. If it misbehaves on yours I would genuinely like to know.
It is free, and it is not going on an app store. Google charges a registration fee to publish, which is not a thing worth paying to give something away. So it lives here: use it in a browser, or download the APK from GitHub. It is MIT licensed, so you can also just take it.

What that costs you, said plainly: it will not turn up in a store search, it will not update itself, and installing the APK means telling Android once that you trust the place you got it from. What it buys you is that nobody needs an account, and nothing sits between the app and you. Something broken? Email me or open an issue.
