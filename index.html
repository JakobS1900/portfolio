01 · The Difference

Jerry off the street builds you a website.
Then leaks your database.

The AI wrote the same code for both of us. The difference is that one of us understands what it actually does. Here's a sample of what I check before anything ships. It's the list I show. There's a longer one I don't.

The vibe-coded build

  • ✕
    API keys shipped to the browser.Supabase / Stripe secret sitting in the JS bundle. Drained overnight.
  • ✕
    Every row readable by every user.Row-level security never turned on; auth "checked" on the client.
  • ✕
    Endpoints with no rate limit.One script turns your usage-based bill into rent.
  • ✕
    String-built SQL.The 1998 vulnerability, generated fresh in 2026.
  • ✕
    Auth with no second factor, sessions that never expire.One phished password owns the whole account.

How I hand it over

  • ✓
    Secrets server-side, scoped, rotated.Nothing sensitive ever reaches the client bundle.
  • ✓
    Authorization enforced at the data layer.RLS / policy checks the client can't talk its way past.
  • ✓
    Rate limiting, input validation, cost ceilings.The abuse cases handled before they're abused.
  • ✓
    Parameterized queries, pinned dependencies.No injection surface, no surprise supply-chain swap.
  • ✓
    Real 2FA, expiring sessions, audited access.Built one for a live clinical platform. It's not theoretical.
This is the public checklist. The full one is the part you're actually hiring: the failure modes I've found in real audits, and the ways I close them. Some things stay in the workshop.

03 · Approach

AI-native. Not AI-dependent.

The reason my work doesn't read as generated is that I treat the model as a fast junior who needs supervision, not an oracle. It drafts; I decide. Architecture, security boundaries, the tradeoffs that don't show up until month three. Those are judgment calls, and judgment is the thing that doesn't come in a prompt.

It also means I engineer around real constraints instead of throwing money at them. On the persona engine I was out of GPU, so I split the pipeline across two machines: a small vision model captions images cheaply on the desktop, and a stronger model reasons over the captions from behind a tunnel, with requests round-robined across hosts to spread the load. That's the instinct a company is really buying: making good engineering fit the budget in front of it.
